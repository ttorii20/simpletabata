Tabata & HIIT Interval Timer

by Takahiro Torii · iPhone, iPad and Android · free

Tabata & HIIT Interval Timer is an interval timer app for iPhone and iPad (iOS 15 or later) and Android (8.0 or later), made by Takahiro Torii. It is free, with one small banner ad: no subscription, no in-app purchases and no account. On iPhone and on Android alike, work time can be set from 5 to 300 seconds, ready and rest time from 5 to 120 seconds, and the number of sets from 2 to 40; up to five timers can be saved, and the cues are a female or male voice or one of three beeps (sharp, soft, whistle). Last updated 28 November 2026, for version 1.8.2 on iPhone and Android.

A simple interval timer. Set the work time, the rest time and the number of sets, and press start. A voice counts the last five seconds and calls each change, so you never have to look at the screen while you train.

It does one thing. There are no workout plans, no videos, no account and no subscription. It has been on the App Store since 2016. Its store name differs by country; in Japan it is HIIT & Tabata Timer - タバタタイマー, and the full list of names is below.

What it does not do

It is worth being plain about this, because it decides whether the app is right for you.

  • No more than five saved timers.
  • No custom sequences, EMOM or AMRAP modes. Every set uses the same work and rest time.
  • No Apple Watch or Wear OS app.
  • No exercise library, videos, coaching or workout plans.
  • The app's text and spoken cues are in English only.

If you want one timer you can start in two taps and stop thinking about, this is that. If you want to program complex workouts, look for a fuller interval timer.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Every feature is free, with no subscription, in-app purchase or account. It is paid for by one small banner ad at the bottom of the screen. There are no full-screen or video ads.

Does it keep running when the screen is off?

Yes. The countdown and the voice continue in the background and with the screen locked. On iPhone (iOS 16.1 or later) the remaining time and a stop button appear on the Lock Screen; on Android the remaining time appears in a notification.

Can I listen to music at the same time?

Yes. Whatever is playing keeps playing at its own volume, and the timer's voice or beep plays over the music.

What can I change?

Ready time, work time, rest time and the number of sets. Work can be 5 to 300 seconds (five minutes), ready and rest 5 to 120 seconds, and a workout 2 to 40 sets, on iPhone and on Android. The default is 10 seconds ready, 20 seconds work, 10 seconds rest, 8 sets, which is the standard Tabata protocol.

Is there an Apple Watch app?

No. The app runs on iPhone, iPad and Android phones. On iPhone, finished workouts can be written to Apple Health if you switch that on in Settings.

Can I save more than one timer?

You can keep up to five timers and swipe between them, so a Tabata, a plank routine and a boxing round can each have their own numbers. Each timer is still one work time and one rest time repeated for a number of sets: there are no custom sequences, EMOM or AMRAP modes. If you need to program complex workouts, a fuller interval timer will suit you better.

Can I have a beep instead of a voice?

Yes, on iPhone and on Android. Under Sound in Settings you can choose a female voice, a male voice, or one of three beeps (sharp, soft, whistle), and tap one to hear it. Beeps carry better across a gym or over loud music.

Can it time three-minute boxing rounds?

Yes, on iPhone and on Android. Work time goes up to 300 seconds, which is five minutes, and rest up to 120 seconds, so three-minute rounds with a one-minute rest can be set directly.

Is it loud enough for a class or a team practice?

The cues play at the phone's media volume, so they are as loud as the phone or the speaker it is connected to. For a hall or a pitch, connect a Bluetooth speaker, and choose a beep under Sound: beeps cut through noise better than a voice.

Does it work without an internet connection?

Yes. The timer, the voice and the beeps all work offline. A connection is used only for the banner ad, anonymous usage statistics and the workout history.

What is Tabata training?

After a warm-up, you alternate 20 seconds of all-out effort with 10 seconds of rest, eight times — four minutes in total. The protocol is named after Professor Izumi Tabata of Ritsumeikan University, whose research on it was published in 1996. The app starts on exactly these numbers.

What data does it collect?

There is no sign-up. The app stores the completion time of each workout under an anonymous identifier, and uses Firebase Analytics, Crashlytics and Google AdMob. It does not use location, contacts, the camera or the microphone. See the privacy policy.
